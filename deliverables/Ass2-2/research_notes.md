# BALROG E2/E3 research notes

## Sources and protocol

I read `docs/Ass2Reasoning.pdf`, the book's Sections 6.2 and 10.4, BALROG's agent and evaluator code, the local README, and the [BALROG paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13543v2). The paper's BabyAI text observations describe nearby objects in agent-relative coordinates, so a remembered distance can become stale after a turn. BALROG's `progression` is binary mission success for BabyAI; Crafter counts unlocked achievements divided by 22.

Development used Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-FP8, temperature 0, text only, 16 text-history entries, one episode per task/seed, and a 32-action cap. The first panel used `goto` and `pickup` at seeds 1–3; the separate door probe used `open` at seeds 1–3. These are exploratory small samples. CSVs contain every proposed and executed action, model output, post-action observation, reward, and outcome. Later custom runs also save exact request/response JSONL.

## E2: supplied planning agent → compact planning

**Observed failure.** The supplied `custom.py` solved four of six `goto`/`pickup` episodes. In seed 3 `goto`, it pursued “go forward” into a wall, then produced invalid action text for steps 11–26; BALROG executed its fallback `go forward` while the view stayed at “a wall 1 step forward.” Seed 3 `pickup` cycled through right turns and stale target locations. Seed 2 `pickup` succeeded, but spent ten actions with the grey key one step forward before picking it up. Its `PLAN: No changes to the plan` text often persisted through these failures. See `experiment_results/e2_supplied/seed_3/.../goto_run_00.csv` and seed 2 `pickup_run_00.csv`.

**Hypothesis and change.** A short persistent plan, facts copied from real observations, and a predicted next observation could keep the goal and current state visible without long free-form reasoning. I replaced the supplied free-form `PLAN` with `SUBGOALS`, `FACTS`, `EXPECTED`, and an exact `ACTION`. The agent retains at most two observation-matched facts and resets them, plan, and expectation each episode. This was a package change, so the trial cannot attribute any gain to one field. The checkpoint is `experiments/checkpoints/custom_compact_v1.py`.

**Result and inspection.** Compact v1 solved five of six, versus four of six for the supplied code, with mean 9.7 versus 16.7 steps and about 6.7k versus 12.9k total input-plus-output tokens per episode. Seed 3 `pickup` changed from a 32-step failure to an eight-step success: after observing the red box two steps forward, the agent approached and picked it up at one step forward. Seed 1 `goto` became a failure: at steps 15–19 it alternated left/right between the same two yellow-key views. Its `EXPECTED` field was vague (“yellow key in front”) and did not prevent the cycle. The full raw response is preserved in each trajectory's `Reasoning` column.

**Reference.** Built-in CoT on the same six episodes solved five of six but used 10.2k input and 3.4k output tokens per episode and made 22 invalid proposals that became fallback actions. Seed 1 `pickup` failed at 32 actions; the CoT text repeatedly described walls or proposed malformed action text. This is a small reference, not a held-out comparator.

**Targeted second E2 trial.** To address vague expectations and turn reversals, I added one stricter instruction asking for a specific observable change and avoiding a turn that restores a recent view. The same six episodes fell to three successes, with mean 17.5 steps. In seed 1 `pickup`, the model predicted a wall-distance change yet issued `go forward` over and over while “a wall 1 step forward” stayed unchanged. This worsened behavior, so I reverted the wording. The exact prompts and outputs are in `experiment_results/e2_compact_v2/seed_1/llm_calls.jsonl`; the transient code revision was not archived as a separate source file.

## E3: feedback-triggered critique

**Failure and hypothesis.** Compact planning's seed 1 `goto` left/right cycle recurred even though both views and actions were in history. A separate critique call, made only after an observation-supported loop or repeated ineffective interaction, might choose a different action. Repeated action or zero reward alone does not trigger reflection. The final code caps reflections at three per episode, at least six steps apart, and keeps at most two short lessons.

**First critique design.** The critic revised the plan and wrote a lesson, then the normal actor chose an action. On the paired six-episode development panel, reflection off and on both solved five of six. Three critique calls added about 590 input and 72 output tokens per episode. In seed 1 `goto`, the critic correctly named the left/right loop but the actor's next output was still `turn right`. See `experiment_results/e3_on_dev2/seed_1/llm_calls.jsonl` at step 4.

**Targeted change.** I let the critique call choose the next action directly while still revising subgoals and adding a lesson. On the same six episodes, this version solved six of six versus five of six off, with two critique calls total. In seed 1 `goto`, reflection at steps 4 and 12 selected `go forward`, changed the repeated view, and the episode finished at step 18. The exact critique prompts and raw outputs are in `experiment_results/e3_on_dev3/seed_1/llm_calls.jsonl`. This is development evidence only.

**Door probe and rejected trigger.** With the compact agent, `open` succeeded in none of three 32-step development episodes. In seed 1, the agent correctly picked up the purple key at step 5, then toggled repeatedly while the locked door remained one step left and two steps forward. The narrow reflection trigger fired late. I tried a broader trigger after two unchanged toggles with a locked-door view. It fired at step 8 but the critic repeatedly chose `go forward` instead of facing the door; this trial solved none of three and used eight critique calls. Its step 8 critique even claimed the locked door was “not interactable,” contrary to the game goal. I reverted the broader trigger. The failed code is `experiments/checkpoints/custom_reflect_v3_door_trigger.py`; the final candidate is `experiments/checkpoints/custom_reflect_v2.py`.

## Interpretation before held-out evaluation

The compact format helped some tasks and cut CoT output cost, but did not ensure spatial reasoning. The reflection critic could diagnose a loop and, when allowed to select the next action, break it in the examined navigation trace. The door experiment showed that diagnosis can still be wrong or operationally useless. Development samples are small and outputs varied between repeated temperature-zero runs, so the held-out panel is needed before drawing a performance conclusion.

## Held-out Qwen BabyAI panel (frozen code, seeds 11–15)

I chose five new seeds per task because development success varied sharply (binary BabyAI progress had sample SD around 41–58 percentage points), while 6 development episodes took roughly 21–27 seconds for the compact/reflecting agent. Every condition used the same five seeds, three task names, 32-action cap, temperature 0, and text history of 16. The naive action output cap was 128, as in Exercise 1; structured conditions used 256. These results refer to the 32-action protocol, not BALROG's default episode lengths.

| Condition | Successes / 15 | Mean progress ± sample SD | Mean steps | Mean input / output tokens | Panel seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Naive | 2 | 13.33% ± 35.19% | 30.13 | 17,909 / 89 | 25 |
| Compact E2 | 6 | 40.00% ± 50.71% | 28.33 | 21,573 / 1,201 | 142 |
| Reflective E3 | 6 | 40.00% ± 50.71% | 26.13 | 19,749 / 1,220 | 146 |

Compared episode by episode, compact planning gained five successes and lost one versus naive. Reflection gained one `goto` success (seed 11) and lost one `pickup` success (seed 11) versus the same agent with reflection off; the other 13 success outcomes matched. Thus the apparent E3 development gain did not carry through as an aggregate held-out gain. The small samples and wide episode variability make stronger generalization claims unwarranted.

**Read trajectories, including successes and failures.** Naive seed 12 `goto` reached a yellow key in four actions; naive seed 15 `open` collected the blue key and toggled the door successfully on action 32. By contrast, naive seed 11 `pickup` alternated left/right for all 32 actions while the same two wall descriptions recurred. Compact seed 11 `pickup` initially cycled too, then discovered the grey ball and picked it up at step 26; its paired `goto` remained stuck in turns. Compact seed 14 `open` remembered “You carry a blue key,” wasted many toggles while the door was two steps left, then eventually faced the door and opened it at step 28. Compact seed 15 `open` remembered the key but toggled a door three steps left and four steps forward until the budget ended. These examples show memory can retain a true fact while the chosen action still ignores the object's current geometry.

Reflection in seed 11 `goto` recognized the turn cycle at step 4, chose `go forward`, and reached the grey ball on the last permitted action. The paired seed 11 `pickup` failed despite a similar critique. In seed 12 `pickup`, a critique at step 4 chose `go forward` and the agent picked up the key at step 9, versus step 23 without reflection. In seed 13 `pickup`, the critic instructed forward movement despite “a wall 1 step forward”; the observation did not change and the agent failed. The model's critique was sometimes useful but not reliably correct.

**Loop and cost check.** `experiments/loop_metrics.py` counts only observation-supported patterns: blocked forward pairs with a front obstacle and unchanged view, alternating turn windows returning to two views, and repeated toggles with an unchanged locked-door view. Across 15 episodes, naive / compact / reflective had 117 / 37 / 51 blocked-forward pairs, 156 / 145 / 22 alternating-turn windows, and 15 / 51 / 48 unchanged-door toggle pairs. Windows overlap within long loops, so these are descriptive behavior counts, not independent failures. Reflection reduced turn cycling but shifted some activity into forward attempts; door toggling remained unresolved. It used 20 critique calls, with a limit of three per episode and six steps between calls. I checked all 15 JSONL call sequences against the episode token totals: every critique call is included. The reflecting panel took 146 seconds versus 142 for planning, so this sample shows a loop reduction at a small additional wall-time cost but no success gain.

## Crafter transfer (Qwen, seeds 11–13)

The small transfer used the same text-only model, temperature, history, and 50-action limit for all conditions. Crafter progression is the number of achievements unlocked divided by 22, not a binary mission result. Naive averaged **10.61% ± 6.94%** (2, 4, and 1 achievements); compact planning averaged **4.55% ± 0** (one achievement on each seed); reflection also averaged **4.55% ± 0**. The naive / compact / reflective panels took 17 / 58 / 59 seconds. Naive made 12 invalid proposals across three episodes, all executed as `Noop`; the structured agent made none.

The Crafter prompt did not transfer the BabyAI geometry lesson. In seed 11, compact planning repeatedly chose `Do` while facing grass and predicted it would collect wood, yet the episode got only one sapling achievement. In seed 12, naive collected sapling and wood, ate a cow, and placed a plant; compact planning kept the subgoal “Collect Wood; Move toward cow,” got wood, then wandered and collected nothing else. The Crafter actions (`Move West`, `Do`, etc.) did not meet the BabyAI reflection triggers, so **zero** critique calls occurred in the Crafter reflection panel. This is a meaningful scope limit of the harness, not evidence that reflection was tested on Crafter failures.

## Limited Qwen3-14B-AWQ comparison (BabyAI goto/pickup, seeds 11–13)

I used the same frozen agent, 32-action cap, task seeds, temperature zero, and text history. This is a six-episode model check, not another development pass. Naive solved **4/6** (66.67% ± 51.64%), planning **1/6** (16.67% ± 40.82%), and reflection **2/6** (33.33% ± 51.64%). Panels took 16 / 125 / 121 seconds and used mean input/output tokens of 9,126/52; 23,903/1,530; and 20,118/1,363. Reflection made 12 critique calls.

Manual trajectory inspection explains the reversal from Qwen3-4B: the 14B planner spent entire 32-action seed 11 `goto` and `pickup` runs alternating the same two views, and did the same in both seed 12 tasks. Its subgoals said to move toward the object and its `EXPECTED` claimed progress, but it repeatedly output `turn left`/`turn right`. In seed 12 `goto`, reflection at action 5 named the two-view cycle and chose `go forward`; the agent then reached the yellow key at action 12, whereas the nonreflecting planner failed. The paired `pickup` reflection also broke the first turn cycle, but later encountered a front wall, received two more critiques, and still failed. Naive solved both seed 12 tasks in 6 and 8 actions. Seed 13 `pickup` succeeded with planning and reflection without any critique. These examples show strong model dependence and that a useful critique can rescue a local loop while a verbose plan can also induce one.

## Limited Phi-4-mini-instruct comparison (BabyAI goto/pickup, seeds 11–13)

With identical frozen settings and code, naive solved **0/6** (0% ± 0), planning **2/6** (33.33% ± 51.64%), and reflection **1/6** (16.67% ± 40.82%). Mean input/output tokens were 16,230/99; 14,615/958; and 18,290/1,181. Panel times were 15 / 68 / 83 seconds. Reflection made nine critique calls. This small panel again shows that planning and reflection effects depend on the model and task instance.

I read the seed 11 failures: naive `goto` repeated forward into a wall and naive `pickup` issued `pick up` while the grey ball was distant; planning seed 11 `pickup` predicted it would have the ball while repeating `go forward` against “a wall 1 step forward” for almost the entire episode. In seed 12, the planner succeeded at `goto` in four actions and `pickup` in eleven. The first planner `pickup` response proposed the invalid compound action `turn left, go forward, pick up`; BALROG actually executed fallback `go forward`, as separately recorded in the CSV. After reaching the key one step forward, the planner tried forward repeatedly, then finally `pick up` on action 11. With reflection enabled, the first five actions matched, but the critic called the unchanged forward attempts a failure and chose `turn left` on action 6. This redirected the agent away from the adjacent key, and it failed by action 32. A better critique would have selected `pick up`; the result is a clear case where a valid trigger and a wrong revision harmed success. In another seed 11 critique, Phi-4 placed fields on a single line, so the strict line parser could not extract `ACTION` and control returned to the normal actor. The critic was not uniformly effective at even specifying a usable action.
